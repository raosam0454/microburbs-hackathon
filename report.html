What will this flat rent for?

Team HanSolo. Weekly rent with and without a car space for 65,777 City of Sydney flats, using only information available by 31 July 2026.

What we found along the way

$378 vs $0Weekly value of a car space for a 2-bedroom flat in Millers Point versus Chippendale.

Parking is a scarcity premium, not a fixed price. In older inner-east and harbourside areas, where street parking is scarce, a car space adds the most: Millers Point $378, Elizabeth Bay $287, Darlinghurst $238 a week for 2-bedroom flats. In denser, newer precincts where most flats come with a space, it adds little: Chippendale $0, Haymarket $0, Waterloo $0. For an investor, $378 a week is $19,654 a year: a space bought for $100,000 would return about 20% gross. The same space in Chippendale returns close to nothing.

Suburbs with at least 30 adverts both with and without parking. Premiums compare drift-adjusted asking rents and are shrunk toward the city figure where data is thin.

Height matters less than people think. Within the same building and bedroom count, flats on floors 41+ ask only 1.8% more than floors 1 to 2. The building and the street set the rent far more than the floor does.

What data supported the result?

The supplied input file (65,777 flats in 33 suburbs), 112,457 past rental adverts from January 2012 to 31 July 2026, and the supplied mesh block home counts for 2021 and 2026. No advert dated after 31 July 2026 was used. The 4,246 addresses outside the supplied City files are estimated from their building, mesh block and suburb neighbours using the coordinates already in the input file. No live listings were used.

How did we estimate rent?

past adverts --> re-price to Aug 2026 with a like-for-like rent index --> remove parking premium (suburb x bedrooms) --> most local evidence: own flat > building > mesh block > suburb > city --> LightGBM correction (floor, building size and age, new supply, location) --> add parking premium back: with and without car space

The clever step is the rent index. It is measured only from rent changes within the same building and bedroom count, so it is not fooled by new luxury stock entering the market. Every old advert is re-priced to the scoring window and projected forward with the last 12 months' trend (0.39% a month). This removes the low bias that comes from leaning on old adverts.

A LightGBM model (median loss, to match the median dollar score) then learns where that local estimate is systematically off. Its training rows come from rolling two-month windows since June 2024: for each window the estimate is built only from earlier adverts, exactly like the real task (30,751 rows). Biggest drivers: gap between the flat's own history and the area (14%), the base estimate (13%), and how old the flat's last advert is (11%). Estimates are rounded to $5 and the with-parking figure is never below the without-parking figure.

How did we check it?

We split adverts by date. Everything up to 31 May 2026 built the estimates; adverts from 1 June to 31 July 2026 (2,726 with known parking) were held out and only used for scoring, mimicking the two-month gap between 31 July and the real Aug to Sep test. The parking column used is the one matching each advert.

What remains uncertain?

Our check ran about $21 high in June to July, so the forward projection may be slightly optimistic if the market cooled. Flats with no advert history and few neighbours (area-only estimates) miss far more. Parking premiums compare different flats, so part of each premium may reflect bigger or better flats that tend to have a space. We do not model aspect, views or condition. The official result on Aug to Sep adverts is the real test.
